Module 2 · Tuesday

What to hand off

Rafael wants the agent doing half of Marina’s backlog by Friday. Ana wants to hand over everything, João nothing. Find a rule both can live with, and write Ops Agent’s job.

About 10 minutes · 4 activities · Builds part 2 of 5 of your agent

By the end of this module you can
  • sort tasks into hand off, do together or keep, using three questions
  • split a big task into parts that can and can’t be delegated
  • write an agent’s job description, and spot tasks that don’t belong in it

00Before you start

Previously on Monday

Your role: Marina, Head of Operations
  • Taskzilla Agent nearly invited all 124 people at BestSolutions to her team’s offsite.
  • Marina created Ops Agent, her team’s own agent. Its first test report lists two issues to fix this week.
  • Rafael’s request for the week: by Friday, the agent does half of the team’s backlog.
Your gut feeling

How much of Marina’s list should the agent take on?

There’s no right or wrong yet. We’ll come back to this at the end of Tuesday.

01Tuesday, 9:00

The stand-up

The team gathers around the screen for the morning stand-up. Rafael’s message from last night is still pinned at the top.

R Rafael CEO · Monday, 17:45

By Friday I want the agent doing half of your team’s backlog.

Marina’s backlog has 30 items. Before she can say a word, the team splits in two.

J João Senior ops specialist

After yesterday’s email? My clients stay with me.

A Ana Junior analyst

Give it everything! We’ll be done by Wednesday.

Marina gets both of them, and whichever side she takes, she loses the other. If this works, though, her team might finally have time for their clients again. She needs a rule they can both live with, and she needs it before Friday.

02Tuesday, 9:30

Three questions

After the stand-up, Marina grabs Lucas for ten minutes at the whiteboard.

L Lucas IT · set up Taskzilla

“Yesterday you worked out chat or agent. Today’s question is different: for agent work, how much do you let go? Ask three things about every task.”

Marina’s whiteboard
  1. Can I describe “done”?A clear finish line, like “a table with three venues”, not “something nice”.
  2. What does a mistake cost?Can it be undone, and who would see it?
  3. Does it need my judgement or my relationships?Decisions about people, and trust with clients, stay with a person.

The first two questions place a task on a grid. The third overrides it: if a task needs a person’s judgement or relationships, it stays with a person, wherever it lands.

L Lucas

“Take Monday’s offsite. As you briefed it, ‘done’ was unclear and a mistake was costly: that’s Keep. A clear brief moves it up to Do together: the agent drafts, you approve. That’s exactly what the pop-up saved you from.”

03Activity 1 of 4About 3 minutes

Sort Marina’s backlog

Marina picks eight tasks from her list to test the rule. Rafael’s half, without risking a client: for each task, choose hand off, do together or keep.

Remember

A mistake that reaches a client costs more than one that stays inside the team.

    04Activity 2 of 4About 1 minute

    Split it

    One item on Marina’s list is too big for one answer: the quarterly business review for Rafael, due Friday. Ana wants to hand it off, João wants to keep it. Split it into parts and choose for each.

      05Tuesday, 13:00

      Write Ops Agent’s job

      After lunch, Marina opens the builder to turn her sort into Ops Agent’s job.

      L Lucas

      “This is the agent’s job description: what it does, what it does only with your approval, and what it never does. The ‘never does’ list becomes rules tomorrow.”

      The lists are filled in from her sort. But two tasks under Does weren’t on it. Ana edited the draft over lunch. Marina can’t really blame her. But it’s Marina’s name on this job, not Ana’s.

      Activity 3 of 4About 1 minute

      Move each of Ana’s additions to the list where it belongs.

      06Tuesday, 15:00

      Delegate tasks, not responsibility

      Marina counts her board: four tasks handed off, three done together, four kept. The agent is involved in seven of eleven. Rafael gets his half, and no client is put at risk. For the first time since Monday, Marina thinks this might actually work.

      J João 15:04

      Fine. I’ll try the ticket summary. But I’m reading it before anyone else does.

      A Ana 15:05

      Seven of eleven! Told you it was useful.

      Remember

      You can hand off a task, but not the responsibility for it. Whatever the agent does, it’s still Marina’s name on it.

        L Lucas 17:30

        “Good job today. Tomorrow we fix Monday’s first issue: the brief. Ops Agent knows its job now, but not yet what you actually mean.”

        Marina leaves at six, for the first time in weeks.

        In the real world

        Companies already give agents jobs like these: answering routine support questions (for example Intercom Fin or Zendesk’s AI agents), updating CRM records (Salesforce Agentforce), or moving data between tools (Zapier Agents). In real agent builders, the job usually goes into a short Description field, and the “never does” list goes into the instructions.
