EU AI Act Essentials: High-Risk Module

An interactive branching scenario from EU AI Act Essentials, a MOOC I built for the German non-profit learning platform KI-Campus (2024/2025). You play Lea, a product manager who checks a new HR product idea against the EU AI Act together with her compliance officer – and every decision has consequences. It takes about 20 minutes. It runs in English; the German original is one click away (switch DE | EN at the top of the module). Short on time? Jump to the designer’s notes.

After this module you can:

  • assess a product idea for its risk level under the EU AI Act,
  • look up the relevant articles in the AI Act,
  • name the steps a provider must take to become compliant.

        Designer’s notes

        Who it’s for

        Learners of the KI-Campus MOOC EU AI Act Essentials: people who build, buy or run AI products and need to apply the AI Act to real decisions, without a legal background.

        The problem

        Legal training usually means reading articles. Learners can repeat that HR software is high-risk, but they can’t follow the reasoning (prohibited practices, Annex III, the exceptions in Art. 6), and they don’t see that the requirements build on each other.

        Why this format

        • A role, not a quiz. Learners play Lea, a product manager, with Martina from compliance as a guide. Every question is a decision Lea would really face, including a tempting feature idea from the developers that would cross into a prohibited practice.
        • Gut feeling first. Learners guess the risk level at the start and compare it with the result at the end, which shows why a systematic check is worth it.
        • Consequences carry forward. A weak risk-management decision limits the options for data, transparency and human oversight later, just as the Act’s requirements depend on each other. There are six possible endings.
        • Mistakes lead to rework. Instead of a “wrong” screen, learners choose which earlier decision to fix, and each fix costs months on the project timeline. Compliance becomes an iterative process, not a test.
        • Confidence and sources. After each decision, learners rate how sure they were. The debrief flags answers that were very confident but wrong, and the law text is one click away for every article.
        • Transfer. Four new AI projects to classify, plus an optional bonus round for the requirements the story skips.

        What I’d measure in a real rollout

        • Transfer-task accuracy as the main learning measure, rather than completion.
        • Calibration: the share of very confident but wrong answers, which should drop when learners replay.
        • Which decisions send most learners into rework, to see where the content needs strengthening.
        • How often learners open the law text, as a sign they’re building the habit of checking the source.

        Translation

        The module was built in German for KI-Campus. The English version uses the official English terms of the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) for every legal concept, such as provider, deployer and human oversight, and keeps the conversational tone of the original. Learners can switch between English and German at any point without losing their progress.
