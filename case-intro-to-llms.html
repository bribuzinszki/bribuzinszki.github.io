Intro to LLMs

Company-internal enablement on large language models, built to meet the AI-literacy duty in Art. 4 of the EU AI Act.

My role
Sr. AI Learning Experience Designer
Team
1 Graphic Designer, 3 Sr. Researchers, 1 LMS Engineer, 1 LnD Manager, 1 Instructional Designer
Audience
Company employees, anyone registered on the Aleph Alpha Academy
Timeline
Oct-Dec 2025
Tools
Google Doc, Figma, Articulate Storyline and Rise, Gemini
Result
100% internal completion rate, 4,7/5 rating, less stress on Engineering

The challenge

The challenge was to achieve a common level of AI literacy in the whole company. While the company was building AI products from scratch (pre-training and post-training included), we had a wide range of AI familiarity in the company, from the experts (RnD, Engineering) to a healthy level of knowledge (Product, Solution Engineering, Customer Enablement) to little knowledge (PeopleOps, Marketing, LnD). Added to that was the remote environment, the limited time in a day for people development, an understaffed Academy team, and a tight budget.

Key decisions

We anchor the common level in the median

I made the decision to build for the biggest group of people, which were people who had already a certain base line of knowledge but were lacking deeper understanding on LLM training and our products. I made this decision knowing that this might exclude people who are starting from zero, and will definitely exclude our experts. The decision was made looking for the biggest impact in the given circumstances. I offered ideas for future complementary peer learning programs for the Engineers and Researchers, and suggested buying and external course for the people who were starting from zero, in order to meet compliance needs.

We will build a course

I made the decision for a course in order to gain flexibility when it comes to time (people were working erratic hours, and had limited time), offer the learning experience to anyone anywhere (remote setting) and to make it easily trackable (compliance, Art. 4 of the EU AI Act), and to easily integrate it into our HR onboarding experience. I knew that picking this asynchronous medium meant, that we will lose the opportunity of close exchange between our experts and non-experts. This was a very real, unfortunate tradeoff that we could have avoided with a workshop format. At the time, a workshop format did not seem scalable under the circumstances.

We won't use videos

The timeline for this 1,5h course was quite tight (4 weeks) with reviews having even tigher timelines due to various vacation plans. This is why I made decision to exclude time intensive videos. This was a gamble, since our online feedback-forms showed us that participants highly valued videos. I offset the missing videos with more interactive elements, where participants had to actively think, decide and build. The course did perform very well, so the tradeoff worked.

The course will be eventually open to the public

This decision was made by leadership and marketing, but had a strong influence on how I designed the course. For example, we built it in German first, since our main customer base was German speaking. We also focused heavily ond the pre-training modules, since that expertise was the one setting us aside from other AI product companies.

Outcome

The internal feedback among the target group and management was overwhelmingly positive. The course achieved a 4,7/5 raiting in our mandatory questionairs, with learners appreciating the interactivity, the accessible language, allegories and metaphors. The interviewed product and engineering teams reported better communication with non-expert areas. Customers and external learners appreciated the expertise displayed.

What I’d do differently

Looking back, I wish I had used more of the coding sections vs. Storyline elements. They felt quickly outdated in their design and were also harder to keep up-to-date than the coding sections. If I could re-design the experience today, I might also try to push harder for a hybrid, flipped-classroom-esque format, where the course is the theoretical knowledge that we then pair with 2-3 team-specific-workshop sessions. I had brought up the idea at the time but decided after consulting leadership that it was not the right moment for this format.
