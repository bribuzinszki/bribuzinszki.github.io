Intro to LLMs

Company-internal enablement on large language models, built to meet the AI-literacy duty in Art. 4 of the EU AI Act.

My role
Sr. AI Learning Experience Designer
Team
1 Graphic Designer, 3 Sr. Researchers, 1 LMS Engineer, 1 LnD Manager, 1 Instructional Designer
Audience
Company employees, anyone registered on the Aleph Alpha Academy
Timeline
Oct-Dec 2025
Tools
Google Doc, Figma, Articulate Storyline and Rise, Gemini
Result
100% internal completion rate, 4,7/5 rating, less stress on Engineering

The challenge

The goal was to establish a baseline of company-wide AI literacy. Because we were building AI products from scratch (covering both pre-training and post-training), familiarity with the technology varied significantly across the organization—ranging from deep domain experts in R&D and Engineering, to functionally proficient teams in Product and Solution Engineering, to colleagues in non-technical roles with limited prior exposure. We had to bridge these gaps while navigating typical startup/scale-up constraints: a fully remote work environment, limited time for internal training, a lean Academy team, and a tight budget.

Key decisions

We anchor the learning baseline in the median

To maximize impact under tight constraints, I recommended designing the core experience for our largest internal cohort: team members who had baseline context but lacked a deeper technical understanding of LLM training and our specific product architecture. I recognized this trade-off meant the core content wouldn't fully serve extreme ends of the spectrum—it wouldn't challenge our core researchers, nor would it offer foundational step-by-step onboarding for complete beginners. To address those edge cases constructively, I proposed complementary peer-learning initiatives for engineering teams and identified off-the-shelf external courses for beginners to meet baseline compliance requirements.

We will prioritize an asynchronous course structure

We opted for a self-paced online course to provide time flexibility for team members working irregular hours, maintain accessibility across remote locations, streamline tracking for compliance (such as Article 4 of the EU AI Act), and enable smooth integration into HR onboarding workflows. The primary trade-off of an asynchronous format was missing out on direct, live exchange between technical experts and non-technical peers. While a live workshop model could have fostered deeper cross-functional dialogue, it simply wasn't scalable given our resource limits at the time.

We will Substitute Rich Media with High-Touch Interactivity

Delivering a 1.5-hour course within a tight 4-week window—compounded by packed review cycles and team vacations—meant video production was out of the question. Since historical learner feedback showed a strong preference for video content, skipping it was a calculated risk. To compensate, I pivoted to interactive decision-making activities, active problem-solving exercises, and practical building tasks. Learner engagement data ultimately confirmed that the interactive elements effectively filled the engagement gap.

We will design for Dual Audiences (Internal & External)

Leadership and marketing decided to eventually release the course publicly. This shifted our structural strategy. We prioritized German as the primary launch language to align with our core client base and we leaned heavily into pre-training modules, highlighting the proprietary expertise that set us apart from competitors.

Outcome

Internal feedback across target departments and leadership was overwhelmingly positive, earning a 4.7 / 5 rating on mandatory post-course evaluations. Learners specifically valued the practical interactivity, accessible language, and intuitive metaphors. Cross-functional alignment improved noticeably between Product, Engineering, and non-technical business units. Within the first three months, the course reached 100% internal completion and achieved an external completion rate that surpassing the standard 30% industry benchmark for self-paced B2B courses. External learners also frequently noted that the content effectively showcased our team's technical authority.

What I’d do differently

Looking back, I would pivot away from heavy reliance on authoring tool elements (like Storyline) in favor of lightweight custom code components. Storyline elements aged faster visually and proved much harder to iterate on rapidly compared to modular code sections. If I were re-architecting the experience today, I would push for a hybrid, flipped-classroom model, using the self-paced course for core theory, paired with 2–3 targeted, team-specific practical workshops. While I advocated for this concept early on, we collectively decided the timing wasn't right; in hindsight, even a light live application layer would have driven deeper retention.
